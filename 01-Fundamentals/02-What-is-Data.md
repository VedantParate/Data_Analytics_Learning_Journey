# 📂 What is Data?

## Definition

**Data** refers to raw facts, figures, observations, or information collected from different sources.

Data by itself may not always have a clear meaning. It becomes useful when it is processed and analyzed to generate meaningful information and insights.

### Simple Example

Consider the following values:

```text
120
150
90
200
```

These numbers alone are just **data** because we do not know what they represent.

But if we know that they represent monthly product sales:

| Month    | Sales |
| -------- | ----: |
| January  |   120 |
| February |   150 |
| March    |    90 |
| April    |   200 |

Now the data has context and can be analyzed to understand business performance.

---

## Data vs Information

Although the terms **data** and **information** are often used together, they are not exactly the same.

### Data

Data is raw and unprocessed.

Examples:

* 500
* Mumbai
* 25
* 12/08/2026
* Laptop

### Information

Information is processed and organized data that provides meaning.

Example:

> Laptop sales in Mumbai reached 500 units in August 2026.

This statement provides context and meaning.

### In Simple Terms

**Data + Context + Processing = Information**

# Why is Data Important?

Data helps organizations understand what is happening and make better decisions.

By analyzing data, businesses can:

* Identify trends
* Understand customer behavior
* Measure performance
* Find problems
* Discover opportunities
* Improve products and services
* Make data-driven decisions

---

# Data in Data Analytics

In Data Analytics, data is the starting point of the entire process.

A Data Analyst typically works with data through the following stages:

**Raw Data → Clean Data → Processed Data → Analysis → Insights → Decision**

The quality of the final insights depends heavily on the quality of the data being analyzed.

This is why collecting, cleaning, and understanding data are important parts of the Data Analytics process.

---

# Key Takeaway

> **Data is a collection of raw facts and observations. When data is processed, organized, and analyzed, it can provide meaningful information and insights that support better decision-making.**
